\textbf{Volume conversion for rod-shaped bacteria.}
Cell length measurements for \Ecoli{} and \Bsubtilis{} were converted to
volume assuming cylindrical geometry with hemispherical caps:
\begin{equation}
  V = \frac{\pi}{4} w^2 \left(L - \frac{w}{3}\right)
  \label{eq:vol_conversion}
\end{equation}
where $w$ is the cell width ($\approx 0.9$--$1.0$ \si{\micro\metre} for
\Ecoli{}, varying with growth condition).
For \Spombe{}, which grows as a cylinder with constant diameter
$d \approx 3.5$ \si{\micro\metre}:
\begin{equation}
  V = \frac{\pi}{4} d^2 L
\end{equation}
\Scerevisiae{} and mammalian cell volumes were measured directly (in fL
and pL, respectively) and required no conversion.

\textbf{Outlier removal.}
Cells with birth or division volumes beyond 3 standard deviations from
the population mean were excluded ($<1\%$ of data in all cases).
Cells with negative or zero growth rates were also excluded.

\subsection{Biological interpretation of fitted parameters}

The fitted parameters are consistent with known molecular mechanisms
in each organism:

\textbf{\Ecoli{} and \Bsubtilis{} ($n \approx 1$--$1.5$, adder).}
The low Hill coefficient reflects the simple, non-cooperative nature
of the DnaA initiator accumulation mechanism.  DnaA protein
accumulates linearly with cell growth until reaching a threshold
concentration that triggers chromosome replication initiation.  The
lack of cooperative binding ($n \approx 1$) produces a gradual,
linear hazard function $h(V) \approx \mu r V/K$, which generates
the adder phenomenology when $K \gg \langle\Vbirth\rangle$.
The large $K/\langle\Vbirth\rangle$ ratio ($\sim 8$--$18$) is
consistent with the observation that the replication-segregation
threshold operates at a scale much larger than individual cell
birth sizes.

\textbf{\Spombe{} ($n \approx 14$, sizer).}
The large Hill coefficient reflects the ultrasensitive Wee1/Cdc25
double-negative feedback loop that creates a bistable switch at
mitotic entry.  This molecular circuit exhibits switch-like
behaviour with effective cooperativity $n \gg 1$, producing a
near-step-function hazard at $V \approx K$.  The fitted
$K/\langle\Vbirth\rangle \approx 1.1$ confirms that the division
threshold is close to the typical birth size, as expected for a
sizer mechanism.  The high $r$ value ($\sim 47$) reflects the
precision of division timing in fission yeast, where the
coefficient of variation of division size is characteristically
small ($\text{CV} \approx 0.06$--$0.08$).

\textbf{\Scerevisiae{} ($n \approx 2.8$, mixed).}
The intermediate Hill coefficient is consistent with the known
cell cycle structure of budding yeast: a G1 phase governed by
Whi5 dilution (moderate cooperativity from multi-site
phosphorylation by Cln3--CDK at $\sim$12 sites) followed by a
post-Start phase that operates largely as a timer.  The emergent
whole-cycle behaviour is a composite of these two phases, yielding
an effective $n$ between the sizer-like G1 ($n \gg 1$) and
timer-like post-Start ($n \approx 0$).

\textbf{HeLa ($n \approx 1.7$, mixed).}
The mammalian Hill coefficient reflects moderate cooperativity in
the Rb--CDK4/6--CyclinD network.  Rb inactivation involves
sequential phosphorylation at $\sim$14 sites by CDK4/6 and CDK2,
but the two-step nature of the process (mono- then
hyper-phosphorylation) reduces the effective cooperativity below
the total number of sites.  The fitted $n \approx 1.7$ places
mammalian cells in the ``mixed'' regime between pure adder and
sizer, consistent with the observation that mammalian size control
involves both growth rate modulation and cell cycle duration
adjustment.

\section{Comparison with existing theoretical frameworks}
\label{sec:comparison}

Table~\ref{tab:comparison} compares the unified equation with
the six most closely related theoretical frameworks published
between 2014 and 2026.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{\textbf{Table S4 $|$ Comparison with existing theoretical
frameworks.}}
\small
\begin{tabular}{p{3cm}p{2.5cm}p{4cm}p{4cm}}
\toprule
\textbf{Framework} & \textbf{Authors} & \textbf{Approach}
  & \textbf{Limitations vs.\ this work} \\
\midrule
Linear interpolation $\alpha$-parameter
  & Amir 2014
  & Discrete map: $V_d = (1-\alpha)V^* + \alpha V_b$
  & $\alpha$ assumed constant; discrete map not continuous
    dynamics; cannot handle nonlinear behaviour \\
\addlinespace
Nonlinear memory SDE
  & Zhang, Fei \& Dunkel 2025
  & SDE + Poisson jump process
  & Inference framework, not closed-form equation;
    no cross-species unification \\
\addlinespace
Stochastic maps
  & Genthon \& Thomas 2026
  & Two random maps unifying phenomenological models
  & Phenomenological; no molecular mechanism connection \\
\addlinespace
PDE boundary conditions
  & Fan \& Lei 2025
  & PDE with different boundary conditions
  & Three controls as different boundary conditions,
    not limits of one equation \\
\addlinespace
Effective potential
  & Kessler \& Burov 2017
  & Steady-state distribution analysis
  & No functional form for the dynamical equation \\
\addlinespace
Stochastic threshold
  & Luo, Bai \& Fu 2022
  & First-passage-time problem
  & Explains apparent strategy transitions but no
    unified equation \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\label{tab:comparison}
\end{table}

The key distinguishing feature of our framework is that a
\emph{single} Hill-type hazard function with two control
parameters ($n$, $K$) analytically recovers all three classical
models as limiting cases, connects directly to conserved molecular
mechanisms (concentration-dilution sensing), and generates
falsifiable predictions that none of the above frameworks can make.
